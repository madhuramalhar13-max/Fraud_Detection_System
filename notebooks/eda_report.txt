DOMAIN INTERPRETATION & EVALUATION METRICS GUIDANCE:
  * In real-world credit card networks, fraud frequency is typically highly extreme
    (often 0.1% to 1.0%), where standard 'Accuracy' is notoriously misleading (a naive
    classifier predicting 'Legitimate' for 100% of transactions achieves 99.9% accuracy
    while allowing 100% of financial losses to occur).
  * In this synthetic dataset, the fraud rate is 32.13% (~2.11:1 ratio). While this provides
    sufficient minority class volume for stable gradient updates without severe minority
    collapse, model performance must still be benchmarked using cost-sensitive metrics:
      - Recall (Sensitivity): Proportion of fraudulent attempts successfully flagged.
      - Precision: Proportion of fraud alerts that are genuine threats (minimizing false customer friction).
      - F1-Score: Harmonic mean balancing Precision and Recall.
      - PR-AUC (Precision-Recall AUC): The gold standard metric for evaluating risk ranking under class imbalance.

================================================================================
                         SUMMARY OF KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================
1. Moderate Class Imbalance:
   Legitimate transactions account for 67.87% (33,933) and Fraud accounts for 32.13% (16,067),
   yielding a ~2.11:1 ratio. While less extreme than 1% real-world fraud, precision, recall,
   F1-score, and PR-AUC must be prioritized over raw accuracy.

2. Uniform Marginal Fraud Risk Across Categories:
   Fraud rates are uniformly distributed across categories: Transaction Types (31.84% - 32.49%),
   Merchant Categories (31.71% - 32.63%), Locations (31.58% - 32.48%), and Device Types
   (31.88% - 32.56%). Higher counts in specific cities (e.g. Tokyo) reflect higher transaction
   volume, not higher intrinsic vulnerability.

3. Overlapping Transaction Amount Distributions:
   Both legitimate and fraudulent amounts share nearly identical central tendencies (Medians:
   $69.40 vs $70.12; Means: $99.28 vs $99.68). However, $0.00 transactions (2 records)
   are 100% fraudulent, confirming authorization card-testing attacks.

4. Near-Zero Bivariate Linear Correlations:
   All numerical features display Pearson correlations with Fraud_Label between -0.0071 and +0.0019.
   Consequently, naive linear models without interaction features will struggle; non-linear
   ensembles (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) and composite feature engineering (amount-to-balance
   ratios, transaction velocity per user) will be essential in Phase 2 & 3.

5. Full Annual Temporal Continuity:
   Transactions span the entire 2023 calendar year (365 days) with stable monthly volume
   (~4,100 txns/month) and consistent fraud rates (31.2% - 33.1%), providing a solid basis
   for chronological train/test splitting.
